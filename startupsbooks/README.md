
# Awesome Startup Books

A community reading map for people who build, validate, launch, and grow startups. It covers customer discovery, product strategy, growth, consulting, leadership, finance, hardware businesses, and operating in India.

> **Curation status:** This first edition is built from a personal working list and notes. The entries below are candidate references, not a claim that every title, author, edition, year, legal rule, or publisher is verified. Please verify each entry before relying on it or recommending it to others. Official legal, tax, and regulatory sources can change; always check the current source.

## How to use this list

1. Start with a problem you need to solve, not with a goal to finish every book.
2. Pick one primary book and one practical exercise for the current stage of your startup.
3. Record the key idea, the decision it changes, and the experiment you will run.
4. Prefer newer editions when the subject changes quickly.
5. For legal, tax, company-formation, and payment topics, use current official sources and qualified professionals. A book is not legal or financial advice.

## Suggested starting path

1. **Customer discovery:** *The Mom Test*; *The Four Steps to the Epiphany*.
2. **Build and validate:** *The Lean Startup*; *The Lean Product Playbook*.
3. **Product and growth:** *Traction*; *Lean Analytics*; *Crossing the Chasm*.
4. **Strategy and operations:** *Good Strategy Bad Strategy*; *High Output Management*; *The Goal*.
5. **Funding and negotiation:** *Venture Deals*; *Never Split the Difference*.
6. **Decision-making:** *Thinking in Bets*; *Thinking in Systems*.

## Practice ideas from the companion notes

The working notes also contain system-design prompts. Use these as practical exercises alongside the reading list. They are project ideas, not books.

- Validate a problem with customer interviews before building a solution.
- Design a simple e-commerce checkout. Cover inventory, idempotency, payment failure, and order state.
- Design a payment integration with retries, reconciliation, and duplicate-request protection.
- Build a small task scheduler or job queue. Document failure handling and observability.
- Design a customer-support or notification workflow. Consider delivery status, retries, and privacy.
- Map a startup's key metrics. Separate input metrics, output metrics, and vanity metrics.
- Write a one-page strategy memo: target customer, problem, current alternatives, advantage, and what not to build.
- Create a simple unit-economics model. State assumptions and test sensitivity to price, acquisition cost, and retention.
